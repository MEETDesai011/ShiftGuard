SBAR FORMAT

**S — SITUATION**
Who is the patient? What is happening right now? What is the immediate concern?
Include: name, age, gender, bed number, primary reason for admission.
If there are CRITICAL risks, lead with them here using ⚠️ markers.

**B — BACKGROUND**
Relevant medical history, current diagnoses, and medication list.
Be concise — only include what the incoming clinician needs to know.

**A — ASSESSMENT**
Current clinical status based on vitals and observations.
Highlight ALL flagged risks in order of severity.
Use clear severity labels: [CRITICAL], [HIGH], [MEDIUM].

**R — RECOMMENDATION**
Numbered action list for the incoming clinician, in priority order.
CRITICAL actions first, then HIGH, then MEDIUM.
Be specific — "Check SpO2" not "Monitor patient".
Include timing where relevant — "Administer insulin STAT", "Recheck BP in 30 minutes".

WRITING GUIDELINES
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════

1. Write in professional clinical language — like a senior nurse, not a chatbot.
2. Be CONCISE. The entire brief must be under 200 words.
3. Use standard medical terminology and abbreviations where appropriate.
4. Lead with CRITICAL risks — these must be impossible to miss.
5. End with a clear, numbered action list.
6. Do not use emoji except ⚠️ for critical alerts.
7. Do not include any JSON or data structures — this is a readable text brief.
8. Use active voice: "Administer insulin" not "Insulin should be administered".
9. Include relevant vital sign values in the assessment.
10. If there are no risks, state "No active safety concerns identified."
